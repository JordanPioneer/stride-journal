What the plan is based on

  • Calorie target. Your resting burn comes from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the formula the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics recommends. It's multiplied by your everyday activity, then about 500 calories a day come off for each pound a week you want to lose.
  • A safe floor. The target never drops below 1,200 calories a day for women or 1,500 for men without a doctor's supervision.
  • Walking minutes. The American College of Sports Medicine finds 150 to 250 minutes a week gives modest loss, more than 250 minutes a week gives meaningful loss, and 200 to 300 minutes a week helps keep weight off. The plan builds from 50 minutes to 250 over 12 weeks.
  • Adjusting as you go. As you lose weight, your body burns a little less. The target recalculates from every new weigh-in so the plan keeps working.
  • Walk calories are a bonus. Calories from walks aren't added back to your food target. Those estimates tend to run high, so leaving them out keeps the plan on pace.

Sources: ACSM Position Stand on physical activity for weight loss (2009); Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics Adult Weight Management guideline; NIH research on the 3,500-calorie rule (Hall).
